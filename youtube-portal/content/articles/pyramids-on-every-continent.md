> Starter article. The 'is this a real connection or just convergence?' framing below is the core move of this whole topic — reuse it freely.

## The observation

Pyramids show up in places that had no contact with each other: Giza in Egypt, Teotihuacan and the Maya cities in Mesoamerica, Meroë in Sudan, and earthen and stepped structures elsewhere. Different materials, different centuries, similar silhouette.

It's the kind of fact that *feels* like a connection. The job is to figure out whether it actually is one.

## The boring explanation (which might be right)

If you want to pile stone or earth high and have it stay up, a wide base tapering to a point is the path of least resistance. Independent cultures solving the same engineering problem can land on the same shape without ever sharing an idea. That's called convergence, and it explains a *lot*.

## The interesting explanation (which we have to earn)

The alternative — that these reflect a shared inheritance, an older common source, or contact we haven't documented — is far more exciting and far harder to prove. To take it seriously we'd need more than a shared shape: matching techniques, matching symbolism, matching dates that line up with a plausible route of transmission.

That's a high bar. We think it *should* be a high bar. This is exactly where careful history and good storytelling have to be kept honest by each other.

## Where we land

The pyramid pattern is real; the *connection* is unproven. We treat it as a question, not a conclusion — and we're upfront about which claims clear the bar and which don't. Check the sources and push back.
